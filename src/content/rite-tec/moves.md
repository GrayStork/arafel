### Footwork

Rite Tec footwork has three families: **fitness steps for rhythm, martial steps for travel and turning, club steps for syncopation.** The fusion is the distinctive part: aerobic patterns executed with martial weight discipline.

| Family | Steps | Source | Function in Rite Tec |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Fitness | Basic, V-step, grapevine, mambo, knee-up, pivot turn | Aerobics in the style of Gilad Janklowicz, Israeli-born host of Bodies in Motion from 1983, whose workouts blended martial arts, aerobics, stretching and circuit training | Constant rhythmic floor; symmetric, countable, repeatable |
| House (directional) | Shuffle, loose legs, crossroads, salsa step and salsa hop, heel-toe, farmer | House dance, from the Chicago and New York underground, built on jacking, footwork and lofting; its roots include Latin dance and martial arts | Turns aerobic patterns into directional travel, the way Latin dancers move across the floor, with real house leg technique |
| Martial: weight shift | Empty and full stepping; bow stance and empty stance | Taiji; Yang family footwork centers on bow, empty, one-leg and down stances | The foot lands empty, weight arrives after; silent, controlled landings that can become a root |
| Martial: turn | Turning stance (轉馬 jun ma): the whole stance rotates as one unit, pivoting on heels, balls or mid-foot depending on lineage | Wing Chun | Pivots where chest, arms and centerline turn as a block, robot-style |
| Martial: advance | Stepping and turning while holding the body's central axis | Wing Chun Chum Kiu, which adds short bridges, stepping, turning and kicking | Traveling hits: the structure arrives intact |
| Club | Heel-toe, kicks, shuffles, stomps, cross-steps | Techno and hard-dance footwork; industrial dance, which runs one move per beat | Syncopation and texture over the base step |

### Projection

Force driven from the legs and the whole body into a chosen direction, the way martial arts project power into a line. It can come out:

- as a sudden, explosive release, in the manner of Fa Jin
- fully empty-handed
- through the rod
- through palm techniques from I Liq Chuan and Wing Chun
- with crossing hand work, fast diagonal lines powered from the legs

**Reels.** Legwork combined with projections. A projection can roll through the hands: it leaves one hand and carries on through the other, as far as the body allows.

#### Ranges

| Range | How it works |
| --- | --- |
| Long | Empty-handed, the body fully stretched, as in contemporary dance. |
| Medium | The range of control and strength. The hand can release fully, as in Fa Jin, or lock instead, which leans towards robot and creates distortion, dissonance and illusions that trick the eye. |
| Short | Also with the rod. The most ritual range: it turns inward and closes the body, following its natural form. |

### The rod

A 3 to 5 kg rod is held as a third point beside the two feet, giving the structure something to organize around. It runs through every layer:

- **Engine:** aerobic patterns and steps performed holding the rod.
- **Transmission:** the rod makes alignment, frame and spiral routing visible and felt.
- **Signal:** the rod becomes an actual staff, and any vertical or horizontal element in the space can play the same role for lines, planes, hits and stops.
