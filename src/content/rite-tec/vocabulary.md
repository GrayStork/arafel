**The movement vocabulary is open.** Rite Tec combines techniques and body-management principles to make elaborate, quick and complex movement sustainable, and leaves room for any expression and experimentation. The terms below are a shared shorthand, mostly borrowed from existing dance, cypher and martial terminology, plus a few named for specific drills.

| Term | Origin | Use in Rite Tec |

**Projection in detail.** Directional projection of force, the way martial arts project power into a line. Variants:

- With a fa jin style release, sudden and explosive.
- Fully empty-handed.
- With the rod.
- Through palm techniques from I Liq Chuan and wing chun.
- Combined with the Cross: crossing hand work with contour and technique that drives power from the legs and whole body.

**Reels.** Legwork combined with directional projections. A projection can roll through the hands: it leaves one hand and carries on through the other, travelling as far as the body allows.

**Projection distances.**

- **Long:** empty-handed, with the body fully stretched as in contemporary ballet or contemporary dance.
- **Medium:** the distance of control and strength, worked through different sets of techniques. Fa jin can be trained here as a fully released hand. Alternatively, instead of releasing, the hand locks, creating distortion, distraction or dissonance effects. These lean towards locking and robot, producing mind-tricking illusions.
- **Short:** can also be done with the rod. This is the most ritual distance: it turns inward and closes the body in yin, following its natural form.
