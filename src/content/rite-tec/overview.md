Rite Tec is a dark-electronic club dance style, a combination of techniques: street illusion styles (popping, robot, mime) on the upper body, a stepping and legwork engine on the lower body, joined by Chinese martial arts mechanics (taiji, I Liq Chuan, wing chun) that send movement from the ground through a connected spiral of bone and fascia, with a theatrical layer from contemporary dance, jazz and butoh for dramatic peaks and dissonance.

### Four layers, one body

| Layer | Body | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The engine | Legs | Aerobics step patterns and techno legwork keep a constant rhythmic floor, one weight change per beat or subdivision. |
| The transmission | Spine, hips, fascia | Neijia principles route force from the feet through the kua and spine as a spiral, so the upper body mainly moves as a consequence of the base, not on its own. |
| The signal | Arms, hands, head | Popping hits, robotic stops, mime planes and isolations make the transmitted force visible as sharp, precise accents. |
| The drama | Whole body, used sparingly | Contemporary and jazz contractions, falls and reaches, plus butoh masks and slow drift, break the mechanical frame open at peaks and drops, adding weight and emotional power to an already intense routine. |

### Name, core technique, core principle

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Rite** | Ritual, repetition and trance: the club as ceremony. |
| **Tec** | Technique, techno and tectonic: plates moving under pressure. |
| **Core technique: dissonance** | Dissonance is a technique Rite Tec reaches for. When used, it holds contradictions at once: machine and body, precision and collapse, a calm face on a violent body or an anguished face on a still one, ultra-slow movement over a fast track. Applied at chosen moments, it keeps the audience from settling. |
| **Core principle: invocation** | Rite Tec's movement aesthetic is ritual. Whatever the vocabulary (hands, face, legs, the whole body), the dance can read as conjuring, invoking or directing forces, a power surge reaching fulfillment, a prayer, an act of zeal. It portrays magic, power, conjuration and witchcraft with high tension and, at times, open theatricality. Together with dancing in fog and the dark ritual mood, this is what makes the club a ceremony rather than a dance floor. |

### Pitch

**One line.** Rite Tec: martial-art mechanics, street illusion and techno legwork, danced as a ritual in the fog.

**Short.** Rite Tec is a dance style built for the dark end of the club: witch house, darkwave, EBM, industrial, techno, experimental electronic and IDM. The legs run a relentless stepping engine. The arms and head cut the air with popping hits and robotic stops. Between them, taiji and wing chun mechanics turn the whole body into one spiral, so every accent starts from the floor. It looks mechanical and feels like trance.

**Long.** Most club dancing rides the beat. Rite Tec is built to *answer* it. The style fuses three traditions that rarely meet: the stepping and legwork of fitness and hard-dance culture, the illusion vocabulary of popping, robot and mime, and the body mechanics of taiji, I Liq Chuan and wing chun, where force rises from the ground, coils through the spine and fascia, and releases at the hands. At the peaks, a theatrical layer from contemporary and jazz dance breaks the precision open with contractions, falls and full-body reaches, while butoh-born masks and ultra-slow drift set the face and the tempo against the body. The result is precise and hypnotic: a figure that seems wired into the sound system, moving as one connected structure. It is designed for the 120 to 140 BPM range of dark electronic music, and it reads from the back of a dark, hazy room.
