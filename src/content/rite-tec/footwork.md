Rite Tec footwork has three families: **fitness steps for rhythm, martial steps for travel and turning, club steps for syncopation.** The fusion is the distinctive part: aerobic patterns executed with martial weight discipline.

| Family | Steps | Source | Function in Rite Tec |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Fitness | Basic, V-step, grapevine, mambo, knee-up, pivot turn | Aerobics in the style of Gilad Janklowicz, Israeli-born host of Bodies in Motion from 1983 ([Hawaii News Now](https://hawaiinewsnow.com/2026/06/10/bodies-motion-fitness-icon-dies-71/)), whose workouts blended martial arts, aerobics, stretching and circuit training ([Honolulu Advertiser](https://archives-new.honoluluadvertiser.com/article/2007/Apr/08/il/FP704080310.html)) | Constant rhythmic floor; symmetric, countable, repeatable |
| House (directional) | Shuffle, loose legs, crossroads, salsa step and salsa hop, heel-toe, farmer | House dance, from the Chicago and New York underground, built on jacking, footwork and lofting ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_dance)); its roots include Latin dance and martial arts ([God's Ballroom](https://godsballroom.com/misc/how-to-do-housing-dance.html)) | Turns aerobic patterns into directional travel, the way Latin dancers move across the floor, with real house leg technique |
| Martial: weight shift | Empty and full stepping; bow stance and empty stance | Taiji; Yang family footwork centers on bow, empty, one-leg and down stances ([Yang Family Tai Chi](https://2009.yangfamilytaichi.com/articles/bow-stance/)) | The foot lands empty, weight arrives after; silent, controlled landings that can become a root |
| Martial: turn | Turning stance (轉馬 jun ma): the whole stance rotates as one unit, pivoting on heels, balls or mid-foot depending on lineage ([Wing Chun, Academic](https://en-academic.com/dic.nsf/enwiki/20547)) | Wing chun | Pivots where chest, arms and centerline turn as a block, robot-style |
| Martial: advance | Stepping and turning while holding the body's central axis | Wing chun Chum Kiu, which adds short bridges, stepping, turning and kicking ([Chaudhuri, RAMA](https://doaj.org/article/0624c5da92604583820095592f968f6a)) | Traveling hits: the structure arrives intact |
| Club | Heel-toe, kicks, shuffles, stomps, cross-steps | Techno and hard-dance footwork; industrial dance, which runs one move per beat ([God's Ballroom](https://godsballroom.com/misc/how-to-cybergoth-dance.html)) | Syncopation and texture over the base step |

**Rite Tec is trained through aerobic forms: a sport aerobics variant built on patterns and moves rather than fixed choreography, worked for cardio, rhythm and breath.** The aerobic base follows Gilad Janklowicz's style of workout (see Footwork).

**The rod as a third point of mass.** A 3 to 5 kg rod is held as a third point beside the two feet, giving the structure something to organize around. It runs through every layer:

- **Engine:** aerobic patterns and steps performed holding the rod.
- **Transmission:** the rod makes alignment, frame and spiral routing visible and felt.
- **Signal:** the rod becomes an actual staff, and any vertical or horizontal element in the space can play the same role for lines, planes, hits and stops.

**Hand barbells, 1 to 3 kg,** carry the same patterns with load in the hands.

**Body practice.** Training also includes:

- Basic qigong.
- I Liq Chuan level 1 drills for dimensionality and body mechanics (directions, alignment and spiral routing, as described above).
- Flexibility drills that work plasticity and dimensionality to rhythm, such as directional popping, where hands or legs hit on the beat.
