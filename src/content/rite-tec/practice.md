### Mind and body coordination

In Rite Tec the mind is always active. It controls the body, reads the situation in the present moment and listens to the body. When the music comes in, that control becomes expression: the body is the engine that turns the music into movement.

This is trained through drills of body coordination that join mind and body:

- **Independent limbs:** different legs and different hands moving separately, each placed and controlled on purpose.
- **Floor work:** coordination drills in positions on the floor.
- **Standing work:** the same coordination while standing.
- **Directional moves:** body parts coordinated through directions in space.

The aim is precise control and coordination of each body part in space, so the mind leads and the body answers.

### Here and now: the rite

**Rite Tec treats the dance floor as a mindfulness practice: techno supplies a slowly changing, repetitive field, and the martial training supplies the attention to meet each change as it happens.**

- **Techno is built for presence.** In hypnotic techno the effect comes from repetition, space and gradual variation more than from tempo. The dancer cannot plan against it, only notice the next change.
- **The martial arts train that attention.** I Liq Chuan builds on taiji and Chan principles of non-assertion, non-resistance and the present moment ([Neigong.net](https://neigong.net/2008/05/27/i-liq-chuan/)); Sam Chin describes the aim as perceiving each moment impartially, staying here and now. In Rite Tec the partner is the track: listen, match, respond, never force.
- **Control of dimensions and direction.** Presence becomes visible as spatial control: every movement resolves onto a known axis (six directions, centerline, diagonals) and arrives on purpose.
- **Control of mass.** At every moment the dancer knows where the weight is: empty and full in the legs, the rod as third point of mass, the root before a release, the fall given and taken back. Controlled mass is what lets precision survive inside trance.
- **The rite.** Repetition, attention and control turn a club set into a ritual in which the dancer is lost in the music and fully in charge of the body at once. That paradox is one more form of Rite Tec's dissonance.
