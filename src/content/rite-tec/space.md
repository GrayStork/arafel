**Rule: straight lines for the signal, spirals for the transmission, verticals for the engine.** Each layer owns a geometry, which is what makes the style read as precise rather than busy.

- **Six directions.** The oldest sense of liu he (six harmonies) is the six directions of space: forward and back, left and right, up and down ([Immortality Study](https://immortalitystudy.substack.com/p/what-are-the-six-harmonies)). Rite Tec uses them as its base grid: every stop and plane resolves onto one of these axes.
- **Dimensional vs diagonal.** In Laban's space harmony, the three dimensions cross at the body's center of gravity and form an octahedron ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Harmony)); diagonals run to the corners of a cube. Robot, mime and popping live on dimensional lines; spirals and crossing hand work travel diagonals. Switching between the two is a core compositional tool.
- **Kinesphere and reach.** Laban defines the kinesphere as the space limbs can reach without changing place, with near, mid and far reach ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Harmony)). Rite Tec phrases often compress to near reach (hands at the chest, tight planes) then snap to far reach on the drop.
- **Centerline facing.** From wing chun: the arms align to a centerline pointing where the chest points, and Chum Kiu trains turning that aligned unit toward a moving target ([Combat Chum Kiu](https://eichendorff21.de/buch/9780988414655/)). In Rite Tec the centerline is the body's aim: turns rotate it in clear steps, so 360-degree club dancing stays legible from any side.
- **Sphere of awareness.** I Liq Chuan calls itself the martial art of awareness ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_FS_Chin)). Rite Tec borrows the attitude: the kinesphere is a sphere the dancer monitors in all six directions, not a stage with one front.

**Effort signatures** (Laban's four factors: Time, Weight, Space, Flow ([UMD Bartenieff exhibit](https://exhibitions.lib.umd.edu/bartenieff/laban-theory))):

| Movement | Time | Weight | Space | Flow |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hits | Quick | Strong | Direct | Bound |
| Stops | Quick | Strong | Direct | Bound |
| Spiral transitions | Sustained | Light to strong | Indirect | Free |
| Mime planes | Sustained | Light | Direct | Bound |
| Leg rebound | Quick, repeated | Light | Direct (vertical) | Free |
| Contractions | Quick | Strong | Direct (inward) | Bound, then free on release |
| Falls | Accelerating | Given to gravity | Indirect | Free |
| Reaches | Sustained | Strong | Direct (outward) | Free |
| Slow drift | Extremely sustained | Strong or light | Indirect | Bound |
| Crossing hand work | Quick | Light to strong | Direct (diagonal) | Bound |
