The internal arts give Rite Tec its organizing rule: **the limbs never move on their own; force starts at the floor, is routed by the center, and arrives at the hands.** Each neijia principle below has a plain biomechanical reading, so the claim holds up without relying on qi language.

| Principle | Neijia term | Biomechanical reading | In Rite Tec |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Power from the ground | 力從地起 li cong di qi | Ground reaction force: pressing and twisting the feet into the floor produces force that the waist turns into horizontal output ([Kung Fu Today](https://www.kungfutoday.com/what-is-the-source-of-power-in-gong-fu/)) | Every hit starts with a foot press; no arm-only hits |
| Whole-body power | 整體力 zheng ti li | Kinetic chain: segments fire in sequence from base to tip | Upper-body accents land with a weight change, never floating |
| Six Harmonies (external) | 外三合 wai san he | Shoulders with hips, elbows with knees, hands with feet ([Tai Chi Notebook](https://thetaichinotebook.com/2016/01/22/defining-tai-chi-chuan/)); cross-body coordination | Arm angles mirror leg angles; a knee lift pairs with an elbow line |
| Silk reeling | 纏絲勁 chan si jin | Continuous spiral through waist and spine; Chen style treats it as the method behind all movement ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silk_reeling)) and research links it to rotating waist and spine for postural stability ([NCCU](https://nccur.lib.nccu.edu.tw/handle/140.119/116335)) | Transitions are spirals, not straight lines |
| Sudden release | 發勁 fa jin | Elastic recoil: tissue loaded under tension releases faster than muscle alone can contract (see fascia below) | Hits: load in the spiral, release at the hand |
| Relaxed readiness | 鬆 song | Removing unneeded co-contraction so tension is chosen, not habitual | Contrast engine: soft between hits makes hits read as hard |
| Expanding frame | 掤 peng | Pre-tensioned structure that keeps shape under load (tensegrity) | Robot poses are inflated, not stiff; arms hold space |
| Empty and full | 虛實 xu shi | Clear weight distribution; one supporting leg at a time | Every step has an unambiguous base, which keeps fast footwork clean |
| Intent leads | 意 yi | Attention and gaze steer posture and direction | Eyes arrive first, body follows |
| Centerline and bone alignment | 中線 (wing chun) | Force is met by stacking bones, not by muscle: Siu Nim Tao aligns the arm bones to a centerline, Chum Kiu points that aligned unit at a moving target ([Combat Chum Kiu](https://eichendorff21.de/buch/9780988414655/)) | Hits and planes are bone-stacked from foot to hand, which is why stops read as locked, not tense |
| Awareness and neutrality | 意力拳 I Liq Chuan; 中心道 Zhong Xin Dao | I Liq Chuan, literally mental-physical martial art, builds on taiji and Chan principles of non-assertion, non-resistance and the present moment ([Neigong.net](https://neigong.net/2008/05/27/i-liq-chuan/)); its training method centers on neutral, moment-to-moment perception ([Ukraine Crisis Media Center](https://uacrisis.org/en?p=60018)) | Freestyle as listening: the dancer answers the track in the moment instead of imposing set material |

#### Bone and fascia model

- **Bones as struts, fascia as cables.** Biotensegrity, a term coined by orthopedic surgeon Stephen Levin, describes bones as compression elements held in a continuous tension network of fascia, muscle and ligament ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tensegrity)). It is a useful model in bodywork and movement education, still debated in mainstream biomechanics. For Rite Tec it explains why a hit in the hand can be driven from the foot: tension carries across the whole network.
- **The fascial catapult.** Kangaroos jump farther than their muscle force explains because leg tendons and fascia are tensioned like elastic bands and then released ([Schleip and Muller 2013](https://www.fasciaresearch.de/publications/Schleip_TrainingPrinciplesFascial.pdf)). Humans use the same mechanism ([Studia Sportiva](https://journals.muni.cz/studiasportiva/article/download/7269/8187)). The rebound in the legs and the hit at the hand are both catapult actions: preload, then release.
- **Spiral routing.** Thomas Myers' Anatomy Trains maps continuous myofascial lines; the Spiral Line wraps diagonally around the trunk and is the anatomical counterpart of silk reeling. Rite Tec's spiral transitions train this line deliberately.
- **Practical consequence:** Rite Tec favors springy, oscillating repetitions over grinding effort, which is also how fascia-oriented training builds elastic storage capacity.
