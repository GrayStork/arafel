Rite Tec is a dark-electronic club dance style: a combination of techniques I have practiced for years. Most club dancing follows the beat. Here I try to answer it. The legs keep a steady stepping rhythm. The arms and head mark accents with popping, robot and mime. Physical principles from Chinese internal wushu connect the two: rooting, the spiral, load and release, empty and full, and centerlines. A movement starts at the floor and arrives at the hands. At the peaks, contemporary dance, jazz and Butoh add weight and contrast.

It is made for dark electronic music, mostly between 120 and 140 BPM, and for dim, hazy rooms. It is still developing, and I keep testing it on the floor.

### Mind and body

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Mind** | Awareness, immersion and focus. |
| **Body** | Fitness and endurance, flexibility, technique, control of mass and an understanding of body mechanics. |

### Four layers, one body

| Layer | Body | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The engine | Legs | Aerobic step patterns and techno legwork keep a constant rhythmic floor. One weight change per beat or subdivision. |
| The transmission | Spine, hips, fascia | Force travels from the feet through the kua and spine as a spiral. The upper body moves as a result of the base. |
| The signal | Arms, hands, head | Popping hits, robotic stops, mime planes and isolations make that force visible as sharp accents. |
| The drama | Whole body, sparingly | Contractions, reaches, Butoh masks and slow drift open the mechanical frame at peaks and drops. |

### Name, core technique, core principle

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Rite** | Ritual, repetition and trance. The club as ceremony. |
| **Tec** | Technique, techno and tectonic. Plates moving under pressure. |
| **Dissonance** | Holding contradictions at once: machine and body, precision and collapse, a calm face on a violent body, ultra-slow movement over a fast track. Used at chosen moments, it keeps the audience from settling. |
| **Invocation** | The movement reads as ritual: conjuring, invoking or directing forces, a power surge, a prayer. With the fog and the dark mood, it turns the club into a ceremony rather than a dance floor. |

### Who it fits

Rite Tec is hard to pick up from the outside. I am no master. I have practiced internal martial arts for more than 10 years in total. For the base principles of Chinese internal wushu, I suggest a dedicated trainer in person: a certified sifu who practices. A video cannot pass them on. Still, anyone who shares a practice with Rite Tec can take something from it. Start from the part that meets your own practice, usually the steps, add one principle at a time and leave the rest.

| If you practice | What Rite Tec can add |
| --- | --- |
| Internal work | Cardio. The same principles carried at tempo, with endurance and breath under load. |
| Aerobics and fitness | Immersion. More than a workout: attention, ritual and trance on top of the steps. |
| Popping, robot and mime | A source of power. The hit as the end of a whole-body spiral, not only an isolation. |
| Club and techno dancing | Structure. Stepping that holds for a whole set, with accents that answer the music. |
| Contemporary dance, Butoh, theatre | A floor. Dramatic peaks set inside a steady pulse. |
| Weights and staff work | Rhythm. Mass moved on the beat, the rod as a third point. |
