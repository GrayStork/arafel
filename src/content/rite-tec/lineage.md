Rite Tec draws on seven influences; each contributes one job, which is the justification for including it.

| Influence | Lineage | What Rite Tec takes | What it leaves |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Popping and boogaloo | Credited to Boogaloo Sam, who founded the Electric Boogaloos in Fresno in 1977 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Electric_Boogaloos)); origin accounts are contested ([Fresno State](https://www.fresnostatenews.com/?p=46711)) | Hits, waves, robot (botting), miming, dime stops, all part of the popping toolkit ([U. Oregon](https://blogs.uoregon.edu/jerkrumpop/?p=48)) | Funk groove and boogaloo leg rolls |
| Hip-hop cypher | Circle-based freestyle culture | The format: improvised, call-and-response, dancer-centered | Battle aggression as the default mood |
| Aerobics (Gilad Janklowicz) | Gilad Janklowicz (1954 to 2026), whose Bodies in Motion ran 42 years and about 600 episodes ([Hawaii News Now](https://hawaiinewsnow.com/2026/06/10/bodies-motion-fitness-icon-dies-71/)) | Aerobic patterns and moves, endurance, rhythm, breath; directional travel carried by house dance legwork | Platform, choreography-for-workout logic |
| Industrial, cybergoth and Tecktonik | Cybergoth blends industrial aesthetics with gothic raver style ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cybergoth)); industrial dance as a goth style developed in the mid-to-late 2000s, spreading via YouTube ([Karampampas, Durham](https://nomadit.co.uk/conference/30/paper/21942)) | Aesthetic, music world, stomps, one move per beat; diagonal and crossing arm patterns and tectonic moves, taken further into complex, martial-looking quick hand work | Pure repetition without technique depth |
| Chinese martial arts (taiji, I Liq Chuan, wing chun) | Taiji (Chen silk reeling, Yang stances). I Liq Chuan, founded by Chin Lik Keong and carried on by Sam Chin ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_FS_Chin)), an internal art based on Chan and taiji principles ([TTOPA](https://www.ttopa.com/iliqchuan.htm)) with roots in rare xingyi and bagua styles ([Kogen Budo](https://kogenbudo.org/guest-blog-i-liq-chuan-%e6%84%8f%e5%8a%9b%e6%8b%b3-by-ashe-higgs/)). Wing chun, a southern style built on centerline and stance structure, usually not classed as internal. | Spiral routing, rooting, empty and full, bone alignment, centerline, turning stance, awareness | Combat application, qi theory as a claim |
| Contemporary and theatrical jazz | Modern dance (Graham contraction and release, Humphrey fall and recovery) and theatrical jazz (Jack Cole, Bob Fosse) | Contractions, falls, reaches: dramatic peaks, level changes, emotional weight | Narrative acting, facial emoting, continuous lyrical flow |
| Butoh | Japanese avant-garde dance founded by Tatsumi Hijikata and Kazuo Ohno; the first piece, Kinjiki, premiered in 1959 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butoh)) | Dissonance as technique, mask changes, slow drift, sudden violent bursts, transformation | Full white body paint as default; techno and dark styling instead, white masks as an option |

**Precedent:** Tecktonik (French electro dance) shows a club-born fusion style can become a named style. It grew in the 2000s at the Metropolis club in the Paris suburbs ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electro_dance)), out of a mix of jump, hard and electro styles arriving from across Europe ([CS Monitor](https://csmonitor.com/World/Europe/2008/0129/p25s03-woeu.html)). Its arm work feeds Rite Tec's quick hand work. Rite Tec differs by grounding the fusion in a body-mechanics system rather than a scene look.

**What is new in Rite Tec:** the parts exist separately; the claim is the integration. Popping usually isolates; neijia usually connects. Rite Tec uses connection to *power* the isolation: the hit is a visible endpoint of a whole-body spiral.
